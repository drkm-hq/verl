### What does this PR do?

**[2/2]** of the V1 in-flight liveness series, stacked on **[1/2]** `[trainer, cfg] feat: in-flight prompt TTL with fresh-uid re-issue (V1)` (branch `contrib/p3a-inflight-ttl`; this diff is measured against it).

Detects dead `AgentLoopWorkerTQ` actors (node loss, OOM kill, crash), re-creates them and recovers the prompts they owned, opt-in. On `main` a dead worker's prompts stay `running` forever and the next `generate_sequences` raises `RayActorError` (no actor in verl sets `max_restarts`; reproduced with `research/vet/E4/repro_worker_death.py`).

- `actor_rollout_ref.rollout.agent.relaunch_dead_workers` (default `false`): `AgentLoopManagerTQ.generate_sequences` catches `RayActorError` per dispatch and never sends that chunk again (Ray cannot say whether the call executed; a re-send could start a second live attempt of the same prompts): the chunk's prompts are parked for the replay buffer's fresh-uid re-issue, a dead worker is re-created with the same construction arguments and node placement rule, and every prompt it still owned is parked too; an `ActorUnavailableError` gets one second-opinion `ping` under the consecutive-failure rule, and a responsive worker is only marked suspect. The replay buffer probes the workers every `VERL_WORKER_LIVENESS_PERIOD_SECONDS` (10 s) with a short `ping`: only an actor death counts (a timeout is a busy worker; "unavailable" must repeat on consecutive probes). The in-flight training prompts a dead worker owned are re-issued at once through [1/2]'s path; they are never charged against [1/2]'s per-step TTL budget and are still re-issued (or failed, past the cap) once it is spent, but they are bounded separately: the liveness probes of a step that report dead workers (death events: one death orphaning many prompts, as after a node loss or during async warm-up, is one event) may not exceed the same allowance (`(max_inflight_reissues + 1)` x `data.train_batch_size`), beyond which the sampler raises a `RuntimeError` naming the events and the prompts ("agent-loop workers keep dying") rather than regenerating or hanging; this also bounds a chunk parked on `ActorUnavailableError` whose worker never answers its pings. Re-creations of one worker index are capped (`VERL_MAX_WORKER_RELAUNCHES`, 5) both in a row without a served dispatch and within one training step regardless of service (a worker that dies after every served dispatch), and fail loudly beyond. [1/2]'s padding-mode hold-back never applies to a dead worker's groups: with no live producer left the sampler's all-failed error is the honest outcome.
- Validation: the dead worker's validation groups are marked `failure` in place (validation prompts are never regenerated) and `_validate` completes. A validation batch in which every group lost its worker (one worker, or a small final chunk) is scored without rows: no reward pass, fetch, decode, dump or clear for it; its sessions count as `acc = 0` wherever another session of the same data source reported `acc`, and a data source whose every session failed reports no accuracy for that validation, only the failed count. A validation without any sample logs no generations (one warning), and the wandb generations table tolerates a changed sample count (a shorter row is padded with `None`, a longer one widens the table), so an all-lost or partially lost validation never breaks the next one's table; row-less prompts from a live worker are counted (`validation/prompts_expected`, `validation/prompts_without_rows`, also when nothing else was produced, so `fit()` never sees an empty dict).
- Diagnostics: the sampling metrics of the validation batches are summed and returned with the validation metrics (`validation/inflight_dead_worker_failed`), and the manager's relaunch counters raised during a validation are drained by `_validate` itself as `validation/...`, so a relaunch during the final validation is logged with it instead of lost and one during `val_before_train` is not attributed to step 1; `validation/prompts_expected` / `validation/prompts_without_rows` are reported whenever row-less prompts came from a live worker (beyond the dead-worker failures) or the validation produced no other metric.
- Flag off (default): the original `RayActorError` is re-raised after an error log naming the worker and the stuck prompts; no probe, no owner map.

What is proven (CPU: the real sampling loops with an in-memory TransferQueue and a virtual clock, the real buffer and manager with Ray stub actors) and what is not: TTL-induced discards per step are bounded by the budget; a step never loses its last finishable group to the TTL; dead-worker recovery rounds (death events) are bounded per step and fail loudly. A worker whose generations never finish and whose pings keep answering still blocks a padded step once the budget is spent, as on `main`; an explicit wait-vs-abort policy at that point is a follow-up.

Boundary: rollout replica health, `update_weights` fencing and load-balancer membership are #8168 and RFC #8380 (`verl/ft/`) and are not touched; both leave CPU agent-loop workers out, this is that half. #6641 makes the legacy `main_ppo_sync` buffer raise on partial rollout groups; no shared code. #8203 keeps `AgentLoopManagerTQ` and its `generate_sequences` (rotated with `_next_worker`), so these hunks re-apply with the rotated index in the owner map (REBASE-PLAN in the package).

### Design & Code Changes

- `agent_loop_tq.py`: `AgentLoopWorkerTQ.ping`; `AgentLoopManagerTQ._launch_worker` / `_init_agent_loop_workers` (same placement rule as `AgentLoopManager`, kept out of `verl/experimental`); `generate_sequences` does one `ray.get` per dispatch, records `uid -> worker index` only with the flag on; `_recover_dispatch` (park the chunk, never re-send; second-opinion ping on unavailable; or log and re-raise); `_orphan_worker`; `_relaunch_worker` (re-create, `ray.kill` the old handle best-effort, streak cap); `_probe_marks_dead`; `reap_dead_workers`. Counters keep their `rollout/` keys; the trainer re-keys them under `validation/` when `_validate` drains them. Workers are re-created rather than `max_restarts`-restarted: in-flight background tasks are lost either way, a Ray restart surfaces as transient `ActorUnavailableError`, and explicit re-creation keeps detection, relaunch and metrics in one place.
- `replay_buffer.py`: `liveness_fn` / `liveness_period_s` kwargs, `_probe_dead_owned` (runs for `val` too), `_fail_groups` in-place variant for validation, the `inflight_dead_worker_*` counters; [1/2]'s step budget (re-issues and exhaustion failures) applies to the expired set minus the prompts a dead worker owned; the probes that report dead workers are counted per step as death events and bounded by the same allowance (`RuntimeError` beyond it; the prompt count is reported with it). `reap_dead_workers(inflight_uids, global_steps)` resets the manager's per-step relaunch counts.
- `trainer_base.py`: `_reap_dead_workers` hook; `_consume_recovery_metrics(prefix)` drained once per training step (`rollout/`) and by `_validate` (`validation/`); `_validate` skips a batch without rows (neutral warning, counted), sums the sampling metrics over its batches, skips the dump when nothing materialized, reports the row-less counts beside the dead-worker counter; `_maybe_log_val_generations` logs nothing without samples. `verl/utils/tracking.py`: `_log_generations_to_wandb` tolerates an empty sample list and a changed sample count (pads a shorter row, widens the table for a longer one). Config: `rollout.agent.relaunch_dead_workers` + `AgentLoopConfig` + regenerated yamls. Docs: the relaunch half of the "Self-healing" section.
- Known limits: relaunch latency = `AgentLoopWorker.__init__` (tokenizer / tools) inside the sampling loop; a retired attempt's engine requests are not aborted (follow-up: worker-side cancel).
